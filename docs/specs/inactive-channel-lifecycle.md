# Inactive channel lifecycle and connection notifications

Date: 2026-10-11
Status: Implemented locally on `feature/inactive-channel-lifecycle`; review and validation recorded in [implementation notes](../reviews/inactive-lifecycle-implementation.md). Not deployed and no production data changed.
Owners: Users for durable account lifecycle; Twitch Outgress for platform observations and effects; Notifications for notification content and receipts; Projector and Ingress for lifecycle-aware delivery.

## 1. Outcome

An account that has disabled the bot, lost authorization, or banned the bot must stop generating repeated warnings and unnecessary Twitch work. An enabled account with no recent use becomes dormant and wakes automatically when useful activity returns. Dormancy preserves the owner's settings and bot-enable choice.

The design replaces daily reminders with **one notification per unresolved connection incident**, and gates work before it reaches Twitch, token warming, settings hydration, or enrollment. Persistent storage holds a compact current state; expiring caches and notification history cannot determine whether an old problem should be announced again.

Confirmed product decisions: include enabled but dormant accounts with automatic wake-up; fully disable normal Twitch-bot execution when the bot is banned; show the detected ban on the dashboard; automatically restore operation on the bot's unban event. Ban/unban control listeners remain available during the automatic disable. An explicit owner Disable still prevents automatic resumption. The 30-day dormancy threshold and operational limits below are proposed defaults. Inactivity does not delete an account, cancel Premium, remove commands, reset loyalty, or change giveaway eligibility by itself.

## 3. Lifecycle and authority

Use a Users-owned SQL child record, one per registered account incarnation, as the authority. Outgress reports verified observations through internal RPC; it does not write another service's database. Projector publishes the versioned read model. Existing Valkey channel fields become disposable observations of this authority.

Separate **desired state** (`desired_enabled`, the owner's choice) from **runtime state** and an optional unresolved connection incident. Do not infer desire from token presence or `registry.Enabled`. Do not overload `sub_state` with dormancy: subscription status and account lifecycle are separate fields.

| Runtime state | Meaning | Routine work | Exit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `active` | Enabled and usable; recent meaningful use | Event-driven operation and demand-driven checks | Inactivity, owner disable, or verified failure |
| `dormant` | Enabled and usable; no meaningful use for 30 days | No speculative status/moderator checks, proactive token warming, or settings hydration | Trusted useful event or authorized explicit interaction |
| `blocked` | Enabled intent remains, but access needs repair | No routine access/status polling or reconnect reminders; retain eligible recovery listeners | Matching unban for a confirmed bot ban; relevant capability evidence or verified repair for other access faults |
| `disabled` | Owner deliberately stopped the bot | No bot runtime work | Explicit Enable by an authorized actor |
| `recovering` | A bounded enable/wake/repair operation is running | Only work belonging to that operation | Confirmed success, permanent failure, or exhausted transient retries |
| `degraded` | Recovery exhausted transient retries; usable access is unconfirmed | No automatic retry loop; show the last failure | Explicit Retry/Enable, fresh capability evidence, relevant fresh grant, or confirmed fleet dependency recovery, coalesced into one bounded operation |

A service-level account ban and deletion override every state. A disabled account may retain an unresolved incident, so disabling and re-enabling cannot reset notification suppression. A dependency outage is `unavailable` read information, not proof that the bot is banned or that the owner disconnected it. Exhausting recovery persists `degraded`; transport/5xx failures alone do not open an access incident or generate a connection warning. Repeated go-live events do not reset an exhausted retry budget.

A confirmed Twitch bot ban is `blocked` with `disable_reason=bot_banned`, `is_active=false`, and normal execution fully disabled. Preserve `desired_enabled` so a matching unban can resume the previously enabled bot. This is distinct from `disabled` by the owner. Store chat restriction and broadcaster grant health independently: an account can be both banned and missing authorization, and resolving either fact must not erase the other.

`is_active` remains a compatibility serving projection during rollout: true for healthy active/dormant accounts; false for disabled/blocked/degraded accounts. Recovering from a blocked state does not set it true before verification. New admission paths use the lifecycle authority, not this legacy flag. Dormancy must not alter Premium tier, account restrictions, or the existing active-account giveaway rule. Record the new vocabulary in the glossary when the design is adopted; do not silently redefine the existing term “Active account.”

Lifecycle mutations, incident opening, and required outbox work commit synchronously in one Users transaction using compare-and-swap. They must not use the existing preference write-behind queue. Change events and cache invalidations follow the commit. Every effect work item carries account incarnation, admission generation, and operation ID; old work cannot resurrect a disabled or recreated account. Admission generation advances when intent, lifecycle eligibility, or account identity changes. Activity timestamps, check metadata, and delivery acknowledgments advance only the read-model revision, so they cannot invalidate an otherwise current operation. Notification work additionally binds to the incident identity and delivery state.

## 4. Dormancy and automatic wake-up

### Meaningful use

Maintain `last_engaged_at` from trusted, deduplicated activity: an admitted stream-online event for a usable channel, a real viewer event that invokes an enabled feature, successful bot work in response to a real user, or an authenticated owner/authorized manager explicitly using channel controls. A bot's scheduled output alone does not sustain activity.

Stream-triggered activity and wake-up must respect the stream-observation ordering preserved in section 7. An online event rejected as superseded cannot renew engagement, start recovery, reset counters, or arm timers merely because it arrived recently.

Passive dashboard/admin loads, `/substate`, SSE reconnects, public page views, raw unused chat, API probes, token refresh, billing changes, background jobs, and repeated delivery of the same event do not count. A stream-online/offline event for a blocked/disabled account does not renew activity or wake it. Capability-specific recovery evidence for a blocked account follows section 5 rather than ordinary activity rules.

Projector exposes a compact, versioned event-interest summary from owner configuration: enabled feature event types and command trigger/prefix metadata. Consumers can reject clearly irrelevant events without full hydration. Configuration changes invalidate the summary. A cold summary permits one fleet-coalesced demand load for that account, cached with explicit empty-result semantics; failed/unknown loads back off for five minutes instead of loading on every message. Cache size and TTL remain bounded. Necessary event processing retains normal deadlines while loading; a cache miss is neither permission to wake every channel nor proof that all its features are disabled.

Enter dormancy after 30 days without meaningful use, provided the channel is not known to have a current live session or an active user-requested operation. Use one indexed `dormant_after` deadline per eligible account, not a scan of all user rows. Coalesce ordinary activity writes to at most one per account per 15 minutes across the fleet; first use and lifecycle transitions write immediately. A dormancy transition checks for newer buffered activity before committing. Unknown initial activity gets a 30-day observation period from migration, never a fabricated historical last-use time.

At a due deadline, use existing live-fact freshness rules. A stale positive live fact may trigger one coalesced live reconciliation for that inactivity window, through the shared budget; an absent/offline fact needs no API call. Fresh live confirmation or new activity moves the deadline forward. If the exceptional check fails, defer for 24 hours, then permit dormancy while preserving subscriptions; uncertainty must not create an indefinite probe loop. A pending operation also defers the deadline rather than leaving the same row at the head of every batch. Wake/admission rules keep a mistakenly dormant live channel usable.

### Wake without losing useful events

For healthy dormant accounts, retain the existing feature subscriptions in v1, including stream and chat subscriptions. The first relevant event atomically claims a wake operation. It proceeds through normal bounded processing with demand-loaded configuration; it is not discarded solely because the account was dormant. Valid subscriptions are not deleted and recreated on wake. Preserve existing event deduplication, ordering, and perishable chat deadlines; do not replay an expired command reply after a slow recovery.

Wake from an admitted stream-online event, a real event that requires an enabled feature, or an explicit authorized dashboard interaction. Opening the home page is a read, not an implicit Enable. Multiple simultaneous triggers across replicas become one transition and at most one necessary recovery/check operation. With valid retained subscriptions and current healthy facts, `dormant` becomes `active` atomically and processes the triggering event. Only a necessary access repair enters `recovering`; a healthy wake need not call Twitch just to prove that it woke.

Concurrent relevant events join the same wake generation and retain ordinary ordering/deduplication/deadline rules; losing the wake claim does not discard a legitimate event. If recovery cannot finish before a perishable event expires, record the normal expiry rather than emitting a late response. Only the first claimant schedules recovery side effects.

Retaining subscriptions has a deliberate cost: dormant channels still occupy subscription and compact account state, and incoming events still require cheap eligibility checks. This reduces idle API/CPU work without promising zero storage or zero event traffic. Twitch supports push stream lifecycle events; subscription cost depends on authorization and subscription type. Monitor the application's reported cost and capacity. [Twitch subscription types](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/eventsub/eventsub-subscription-types/#stream-online), [subscription limits](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/eventsub/manage-subscriptions/#subscription-limits).

If a dormant subscription is lost, process its revocation or fleet transport-recovery event through a bounded repair operation. Do not invent a polling loop to discover activity. A missing wake subscription is an observable degraded state with explicit Retry; reconnect/Enable is the fallback if no event can arrive. A dormant account whose authorization fails becomes blocked.

## 5. Blocked and disabled accounts

On a verified access failure, commit the incident and block admission before scheduling cleanup. Stop moderator checks, `stream_status` escalation, token prewarm, speculative enrollment, bot-only settings hydration, and loyalty baseline work. Recheck admission at execution as well as enqueue, including delayed jobs and batch children. Fleet/service bans, trial provenance, and existing output safety rules remain stronger gates.

Remove unnecessary bot-processing EventSub subscriptions through durable, bounded reconciliation; retain subscription ownership until Twitch confirms deletion or absence. For a blocked account that still desires enablement, retain eligible recovery-specific subscriptions described below. A deliberate disable removes recovery interest too. Shared stream subscriptions needed by a separately enabled Discord feature remain, but their events cannot trigger Twitch-bot warming, hydration, checks, or wake-up. Subscription interest must distinguish normal processing, recovery, and independent consumers; a blanket delete must not break another enabled integration or remove the only automatic recovery signal. Trial channels retain their separate lifecycle and output prohibition.

No periodic recovery probe is scheduled for a confirmed bot ban, revoked grant, or dead grant. A generic 403 is not sufficient evidence for the specific message “The bot is banned.” Preserve a neutral access error unless Twitch gives an unambiguous ban signal. Expired but refreshable credentials, 429, network failures, and 5xx are not permanent user faults.

### Bot ban and unban contract

Subscribe to `channel.ban` and `channel.unban`, version 1, for each eligible broadcaster. First filter for the exact broadcaster and `event.user_id == configured_bot_id`; events affecting other viewers cause no lifecycle writes, checks, warnings, or recovery work. The subscriptions cover the channel, so this early filter is required.

| Accepted signal | Durable transition and behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Permanent `channel.ban` for our bot | Record the ban, advance admission generation, disable normal execution immediately, open/reuse the connection incident, and invalidate dashboard state. Stop chat/commands/modules, timers, AutoMod actions, watchtime awards, scheduled output, and channel-owned Twitch API effects. Retain both control listeners. |
| `chat_user_banned` revocation for our current bot chat subscription | Apply the same stop as a fallback when the ban notification is absent. Preserve the affected subscription ID/generation. A known timeout fact for the same restriction remains a timeout rather than becoming a permanent ban. |
| New matching `channel.unban` | Persist the unban fact, cancel obsolete ban cleanup, and enter one bounded `recovering` operation if desired enablement remains true. Restore required subscriptions and verify other access conditions, then set `active`/`is_active=true` automatically. |
| New ban during recovery | Advance the generation and keep execution disabled. The older unban operation and its completion cannot reactivate the bot. |
| Owner Disable before/during unban recovery | Preserve manual disablement and cancel automatic resume. An unban updates known chat restriction but does not enable the bot. |

Ban disablement cancels pending timers and runtime jobs; Outgress checks the current admission generation before every effect, including batch children. Jobs queued before the ban are not released when the bot is unbanned. Runtime settings and user data remain stored. A Twitch call already accepted before detection cannot be undone, but no newly admitted effect may bypass the recorded ban. Normal subscription cleanup must leave the ban/unban listeners alive and delete only obsolete subscription IDs.

Twitch also uses `channel.ban` for timeouts. If `is_permanent=false` and `ends_at` is valid, store a temporary restriction and show **Timed out until …**. Suppress normal execution until a matching unban or one generation-fenced expiry/recovery operation at that deadline; do not assume Twitch sends an unban on natural timeout expiry. An extension or later permanent ban invalidates the old expiry job. Missing/invalid timeout details remain an explicit unknown chat restriction, not an invented permanent ban. [Twitch Channel Ban](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/eventsub/eventsub-subscription-types/#channel-ban).

Recovery triggers:

- **Bot banned:** `channel.unban` for this bot is the normal automatic recovery trigger. Go-live, incoming chat, another viewer's unban, a raid, a follow, or merely pressing Enable cannot clear a confirmed ban. Explicit Retry remains a bounded verification fallback after the owner reports an unban whose event was missed; it must verify restored access rather than just flipping the flag.
- **Authorization revoked/dead:** a successful saved OAuth grant or verified authorization-grant event may attempt recovery when desired enablement remains true and no independent chat ban remains. When still banned, a grant restores control-listener eligibility only. An unrelated token/profile request succeeding does not prove channel access.
- **Deliberately disabled:** only explicit Enable. Login, OAuth renewal, stream events, admin inspection, or cache reconstruction never override that choice.
- **Operator repair:** explicit, authorized action with actor and operation ID; same fencing and retry rules as user repair.

### Recovery listeners and evidence

Both control subscriptions require the broadcaster's `channel:moderate` grant. Create them on the existing Conduit with the app token and broadcaster authorization, independently from the bot account's chat subscription or moderator seat. Provision both during normal enrollment so they are already listening before a ban; retain both during ban disablement and recovery so a re-ban is observed. The inspected [subscription catalog](../../app/twitch/outgress/internal/twitch/eventsub.go) and default [dashboard OAuth scopes](../../web/dashboard/src/lib/server/oauth.ts) do not yet include this pair/scope; reconcile with the implementation baseline before adding them. Add the scope to defaults and audit deployed `DASHBOARD_LOGIN_SCOPES` overrides, which replace the defaults. Existing grants need explicit re-consent to enable the new listeners; do not interrupt otherwise healthy bot use merely for a missing new scope. Expose listener permission/health until consent and subscription creation succeed. [Twitch Channel Unban](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/eventsub/eventsub-subscription-types/#channel-unban), [Conduit authorization](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/eventsub/handling-conduit-events/).

A chat ban can revoke the chat subscription; unbanning is not sufficient to assume that it spontaneously resumes delivering. The recovery listener supplies the signal to recreate/verify required subscriptions once. If its grant is revoked, subscription is missing, or the unban happens while the listener is unavailable, show that automatic recovery is unavailable and keep Enable/Retry; do not compensate with repeated go-live probes. Integration verification must confirm the broadcaster-authorized listener remains usable when the bot account is banned. [Twitch chat subscription revocation](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/chat/irc-migration/).

Ingress admits recovery evidence to a narrow control path before ordinary blocked-channel processing is suppressed. Carry trusted subscription ID/type/version/condition, bot identity where applicable, subscription ownership generation, original Twitch message ID/timestamp, transport/trial provenance, and account incarnation. Keep provider time separate from local ingestion time and record the failure's evidence boundary. The current flattened lane, authorization and revocation metadata do not retain all these fields; extend the recovery contract rather than inferring access from chat text or local receive time. Trial/alternate-identity events, revoked subscription IDs, replayed messages, events predating the block, evidence for a previous account incarnation, and legacy envelopes lacking evidence metadata cannot recover this account. Inspect eligibility through bounded cached control state; do not hydrate configuration, warm tokens, or call Users for every arriving blocked-channel message.

An event proves only its own capability. Incoming chat can initiate bounded repair of an ambiguous access failure, but cannot override a confirmed ban or establish every OAuth scope/permission. The confirmed-ban flow waits for the matching unban or verified explicit repair. No diagnostic chat message is sent. Preserve the normal deadline for a triggering user command; do not execute it until admission is restored or replay it late.

Persist a bot-only moderation watermark even when an unban arrives before its delayed ban. Keep source action time (`banned_at` where present), original provider message time/ID, restriction kind/expiry, and subscription ownership generation. Arrival time and lexicographic message IDs are not action order; the live-race PR in section 7 also shows why a newer message timestamp alone is not conclusive about Twitch state. When chronology is ambiguous, remain stopped and permit one coalesced reconciliation for that ambiguity instead of guessing active or launching repeated checks.

Accepting an unban retires the pre-unban chat-subscription generation. A later `chat_user_banned` revocation for that retired subscription records its absence but cannot overwrite a newer accepted unban/recovery. A genuinely new ban or a rejection of the new subscription still stops the bot. Retain bounded subscription tombstones for the accepted replay window, and carry subscription identity/provider metadata through the currently lossy revocation DTO. Use the same generation fences for cleanup and recovery, so delayed cleanup cannot delete recreated subscriptions.

Persist the latest accepted recovery evidence and subscription/grant generation with the incident, and consume each evidence generation once. Repeated unban deliveries or subsequent chat messages from the same already-tested subscription generation cannot restart a failed recovery budget. A genuinely newer unban/grant/subscription transition may start another operation. Recheck desired enablement, service bans, deletion and admission generation before every effect; even convincing recovery evidence never overrides a deliberate Disable. This adds event-triggered verification, not periodic polling or another warning.

Confirm the required grant and subscriptions/capabilities before closing the incident. Failed repair remains the same incident and updates the inline result; it sends no new warning. A newer disable wins over an in-flight recovery. Effects already accepted by Twitch may need compensating cleanup, but their stale completion cannot re-enable the account.

Twitch requires startup and hourly token validation for maintained OAuth sessions. Dormancy must not disable that requirement or confuse validation with optional channel-status polling. Stop speculative broadcaster refresh/probes and evict idle process caches, while retaining required validation for sessions still maintained. Clearing a cache is not ending an OAuth session; reducing those validations requires a separate session-lifecycle change. App/bot credentials shared by active channels continue their required maintenance. [Twitch token validation](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/authentication/validate-tokens/).

## 6. One warning per connection incident

An incident is one continuous period of unusable channel access. Store its identity independently from notification rows: `connection:<account_id>:<account_incarnation>:<incident_sequence>`. The account ID is required because a creation-time incarnation is not globally unique. Neither a calendar date nor a pod ID belongs in that key.

Open an incident only on a verified usable-to-blocked transition, or a first authoritative failure when no unresolved incident exists. One Users transaction records the new incident and one notification-outbox operation. Concurrent failures and redelivery converge on the same incident. If a stronger cause arrives, update the current incident/banner without creating another bell item or marking an existing item unread again.

Send one localized dashboard warning with the correct repair action. Remove recurring go-live reconnect chat messages entirely; these connection incidents do not send email or unsolicited chat. Reading, dismissing, deleting, or expiring the warning never clears the incident. The dashboard's persistent status/banner continues to show the problem after notification history expires.

Only confirmed recovery resolves the incident. A later genuine failure may open a new incident; additionally cap new connection bell warnings at one per account per rolling 24 hours to avoid noisy flapping. A new incident within that cooldown is marked suppressed, with the current problem still visible in the status panel. Do not schedule a delayed reminder when the cooldown expires.

Notifications enforces this cap against actual content creation time, in the same transaction as content and delivery-receipt insertion, using a locked per-account/incarnation last-creation record. A cooldown refusal is a terminal suppressed receipt returned to Users. Incident opening time and an asynchronous acknowledgment timestamp cannot enforce the cap, because delivery may be delayed.

The outbox retries delivery failures with the same incident key and fixed original `expires_at`. Notifications atomically records a unique delivery receipt alongside content, and returns the previous outcome on retries even if an administrator subsequently retracts the content. Retain that delivery receipt/tombstone through the original content expiry or seven days after the delivery deadline, whichever is later. A crash after insert but before Users records acknowledgment therefore cannot recreate a retracted warning.

Notification requests carry the account incarnation, incident identity, and original delivery deadline. Retry the initial nudge for at most 24 hours; afterward mark it expired and rely on the persistent banner. Validate current delivery eligibility through Users before a new insert; unavailable authority defers delivery, and expired deadlines or known canceled/resolved incidents are rejected. Users and Notifications do not share a transaction: a concurrent recovery/disable/erasure after that validation can race an insert. The same Users transition cancels pending delivery and durably requests retraction. Notifications orders retraction and insertion by the incident key, persisting a tombstone if retraction arrives first, so reconciliation removes any raced content without permitting its resurrection. Neither path resets the durable incident suppression state. Delivery failure raises an operational metric, not another user notification.

## 7. Scheduling and API budgets

Use the existing system lane for bounded effects. Users persists only work that must converge: enable/repair, subscription removal, lifecycle projection repair, and notification delivery. Outgress claims eligible work through Users RPC. A Valkey due index may accelerate this, but SQL remains sufficient to resume after cache loss.

| Work | Proposed scheduling contract |
| --- | --- |
| Unknown live state | Preserve Projector's cold escalation on real eligible demand, with a shared in-flight claim. Keep this separate from known-offline handling below. |
| Known-offline live demand | Preserve PR #1246's fleet-wide five-minute `live:demand:` window. An applied offline reserves the initial window so its scheduled confirmation handles the first look. |
| Applied offline confirmation | Preserve checks at 2 and 10 minutes after the applied observation, even without a new command. Skip a stage if the channel is live again. These are bounded correctness checks, not a periodic account poll; blocked/disabled accounts cannot execute them. |
| Moderator status | Use the existing 24-hour freshness ceiling and per-channel fleet claim for demand-driven checks. Go-live honors freshness and lifecycle admission. A shared cache of the bot's complete moderated-channel list is a separate optimization, not required for stopping blocked/dormant work. |
| Blocked recovery evidence | One operation per fresh relevant evidence generation; reuse the bounded repair budget. No recovery on generic activity, duplicate evidence, or elapsed time alone. |
| Dormancy deadline | Indexed due-only selection, 200 rows per batch, maximum one pass per minute. Revalidate recent use and current lifecycle before transition. No routine Twitch call per row; only the bounded stale-positive-live exception in section 4. |
| Enable/repair transient failure | At most three claimed attempts per operation, using the existing Helix budgets and minute-scale persisted backoff. No extra broker retry loop multiplying these attempts. After exhaustion, enter `degraded` with no routine probe; a fresh authorized trigger starts another bounded operation. |
| Subscription removal / durable delivery | Bounded indexed batches with persisted progress. Notification delivery obeys its 24-hour deadline. Incomplete subscription cleanup remains pending at no more than one attempt per 24 hours after the initial backoff, with an operational alert. This is cleanup, not an access check. |

Reuse existing system Helix budgets, timeouts, and concurrency caps. Lease expiration permits another worker to continue, but admission-generation/operation fencing rejects stale results. A failed storage write after a successful API call must retry persistence or reconcile from saved results rather than blindly paying for the API call again.

When lifecycle authority is unavailable, skip optional background work and return cached status with an explicit freshness/unavailable indication. Registered channels missing from a cache require a bounded authority lookup; absence must never default to enabled. Preserve explicitly supported unregistered/trial behavior through separate admission rules.

### Compatibility with the Twitch live race fix

The user specifically requested preservation of [PR #1246 — fix(live): recheck stream.offline against Helix and version the projected live flag](https://github.com/AdamOusmer/ItsBagelBot/pull/1246), merged as `82eecf569bf0f309ef9c129554c8cdfd4c0da4fa` from head `3278e53db703ec081fdfa821b9ea6105036a8043`. The implementation baseline contains that merge, and retains its later regression tests.

The defect was a delayed offline event arriving after a new online event with a *newer* Twitch message timestamp. Timestamp comparison alone could not detect the old stream ending. The bot stayed offline because known-offline reads never verified that verdict. This spec must not remove the correction path while reducing idle work.

Required invariants:

1. Preserve the two confirmation stages and known-offline demand contract above. The first Helix sample may still lag behind a restart; a negative first sample does not remove the second stage. Confirmation timing is not replaced by the enrollment retry schedule. Confirmations and expiry callbacks retain their fleet-wide claims; claim/version key expiry never recursively schedules checks.
2. Healthy enabled channels keep these guarantees, including a dormant channel handling a genuine new stream event or waking for live-only demand. A dormant flag alone cannot suppress a necessary correction for that activity. In the absence of such activity, dormancy creates no recurring checks. Ban/manual-disable gates apply at both scheduling and execution; a check already in flight also needs a current-generation write/admission guard before its result can restore serving.
3. Preserve the original stream observation version through decoding, atomic Projector `live`/`live_ver` folds, live RPC replies, and Sesame restoration. Older refused observations cause no go-live side effects. `LiveReply.Version` is reused; replacing it with the current time can let stale projected live state defeat a newer offline clear.
4. Both live and stream-info reads must choose the newest verdict between projected fields and the versioned `live:<id>`/`live:ver:<id>` keys, including Helix corrections. Projector still must not write the flat `live:<id>` key: projected status alone cannot admit watchtime.
5. A stream-offline handler may disarm timers or arm confirmations only when `ClearLive` applied. A stale offline cannot disarm a newer stream's timers. Ban cleanup separately stops timers because account admission was revoked; it does not invent a stream-offline observation or falsify Twitch live state.
6. Account incarnation/admission generation is an additional axis of ordering; it cannot replace stream-observation versions. A live verdict means the stream is live, not that the bot is authorized/enabled. Timer rearm, watchtime, cache restoration and outputs require both applicable live state and current lifecycle admission. Preserve the normal idempotent reconciliation that observes a positive Helix correction and rearms eligible timers; the merged baseline has a one-minute timer reconciler. Gate each rearm/award so that reconciliation cannot restart a banned or manually disabled bot. The combined lifecycle behavior requires additional coverage beyond the original PR's live-state tests.
7. Registry pruning affects lifecycle-owned cache/index keys only. Preserve the stream version tombstone's existing 48-hour protection, current confirmation stages, and demand/recheck claims. Explicit ban/disable can cancel its pending stages with generation fencing, but generic cold-cache cleanup cannot remove race protection for a healthy channel. No plain `SET`/`DEL` may replace version-aware live writes.
8. After unban recovery succeeds mid-stream, perform the existing bounded live seed because a newly created subscription may not receive an online event for the stream already in progress. Feed its verdict through the existing version-aware writer and normal eligible timer/watchtime reconciliation. Never synthesize an online event stamped with now from old cached data, replay missed commands, or grant watchtime for the banned interval.

Keep the original regressions (or their later equivalent exported-seam coverage) and add the lifecycle cases in section 11. Required named coverage from the PR includes `TestOfflineRightAfterOnlineIsRecheckedAgainstTwitch`, `TestOfflineStillUnconfirmedIsRecheckedAgain`, `TestSupersededOfflineArmsNoConfirmation`, `TestConfirmationIsSkippedOnceLiveAgain`, `TestOfflineEventDefersDemandRecheck`, `TestExpiryRechecksAreClaimedOncePerWindow`, `TestExpiredKeysThatRecheck`, `TestColdReadOfProjectedLiveState`, `TestColdReadRestoresOnlyAtTheProjectedVersion`, `TestStreamLiveFoldRefusesStaleEvents`, `TestStreamLiveFoldReportsThePriorState`, `TestStreamLiveReadTakesTheNewestVerdict`, `TestLiveReadsFollowATwitchRestoredStream`, `TestStaleStreamOfflineLeavesANewerLiveProjection`, and `TestStaleOfflineLosingToNewerOnlineIsIgnored`.

## 8. Storage contract

### Durable records

| Store | Fields / bounds | Retention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Users current `lifecycle` JSON and indexed due columns | Account incarnation; desired enablement; runtime state; independent grant health and bot chat restriction; admission generation and read-model revision; engagement and dormancy deadline; current incident and notice outcome; recovery operation and evidence watermarks; optional timeout deadline | One compact current record on the existing User row. The incident marker and bot moderation fact remain after notification expiry and cache loss. Delete through account erasure. |
| Subscription ownership records | IDs/type/generation for current normal and ban/unban subscriptions; listener permission/health; retired chat-subscription IDs tied to account incarnation | Keep current ownership while the subscription exists. Bound retired tombstones by the accepted event replay window; events beyond that window cannot change lifecycle. Never retain other viewers' moderation events in this lifecycle store. |
| Users reconciliation/outbox fields | Current operation ID, expected generation, attempt count, next attempt and claim lease in the current lifecycle record; at most eight pending incident retractions | Superseded work is canceled. A full retraction queue defers recovery completion rather than losing a retraction. Separate erasure tombstones survive User deletion and are pruned seven days after completion. |
| Notifications | Existing content/recipient/receipt data plus typed category `connection`, account incarnation and incident key for new connection notices | Keep the existing 90-day global default; read/peek visibility rules remain. Original expiry never extends on replay. |
| Notifications delivery receipts | Unique incident key, account ID/incarnation, accepted/suppressed/retracted outcome, original deadline and expiry; independent of the visible parent | Retain through the later of original content expiry and delivery deadline + 7 days; then batch-delete. Users' compact suppression state outlives these receipts. |
| Notifications connection-delivery guard | One row per account/incarnation with last successful connection-notice creation time; locked during new delivery | Remove after its cooldown has elapsed and no pending delivery requires it, or through incarnation-fenced erasure. |

Only the latest observation is needed in lifecycle storage; do not append a database record for every skipped event, poll, activity ping, or retry. Use aggregate metrics for repeated attempts. Store normalized reason codes and short sanitized error summaries, never OAuth secrets, chat text, or raw upstream response bodies in lifecycle/outbox diagnostics.

Use a unique account/incarnation constraint; indexed due queries for `(runtime_state, dormant_after, account_id)` and `(operation_state, next_attempt_at, id)`; and unique current operations by account/incarnation/kind. Verify query plans on representative cardinalities. Do not repeatedly call the existing registry `List` to find inactive or due accounts.

### Caches and registry

Project only hot/needed state. Expire dormant/disabled/blocked registry hashes after 7 days without actual authorized use; remove membership from the hot-channel index when expiring/evicting. Use a sorted hot index scored by eviction deadline and bounded due pruning, with incarnation/generation-aware removal so pruning cannot remove a newly refreshed entry. Expiry notifications may accelerate pruning but are not its only trigger. Reads must not indefinitely renew cold entries merely because an admin list is open. Use paginated Users queries for the account inventory, and bounded hot-channel/due indexes for runtime work. `SMEMBERS` followed by one read per historical ID is not an acceptable scheduler or admin inventory implementation.

Lease/claim keys have explicit short TTLs; due entries are removed on cancellation/completion. Rebuild projections lazily from the durable row and reconstruct due work in bounded batches. A complete Valkey loss must not reopen an incident, resend its warning, or enroll all registered channels. Compacting registry fields is safe only after these fallback semantics are deployed.

### Notification cleanup and account deletion

Add an expiry index beginning with `expires_at` and a deterministic ID tie-breaker. Delete expired parents in batches of 500 within the existing maintenance RPC budget, returning progress and whether more remain. Resume until drained through bounded follow-up invocations; retain cascade deletion of read receipts. Do not delete read receipts merely because their visibility cutoff passed while the parent can still be shown; that can make old content appear unread again. Delivery receipts are separate and follow their own indexed retention deadline. Monitor oldest expired row and cleanup failures. Under healthy operation, expired content should be physically removed within 48 hours; the daily CronJob manifest alone is not evidence that this is happening.

Inventory legacy null-expiry rows by notification category. Backfill expiry only for positively identified automated connection warnings using `created_at + 90 days`; preserve deliberate permanent announcements. Do not infer category from translated title text.

On account erasure, propagate an incarnation-fenced deletion to Notifications and Outgress: remove that incarnation's direct notifications and read receipts, runtime caches/index membership, token cache entries, and pending operations; reconcile owned subscriptions. Shared broadcast parents remain for other recipients. Cross-service cleanup must be resumable from a deletion tombstone even after the Users row is gone. A later registration with the same Twitch ID cannot inherit old direct notifications or old work. The current numeric-ID-only notification schema needs this migration explicitly.

Cancel ordinary account work, but transfer necessary subscription-removal and notification-retraction operations to the erasure tombstone until acknowledged. Save the exact subscription IDs and their owning incarnation before deleting runtime state. Old cleanup may delete only those IDs after ownership verification; it must never delete all subscriptions by broadcaster ID after a replacement account enrolls. Keep only the minimal delivery/erasure tombstones needed to reject bounded stale work; erase notification content and user read history immediately through the deletion workflow.

Steady-state storage is proportional to registered accounts plus retained real incidents and pending operations, rather than repeated checks. Example, assuming one chronic incident and one go-live on each of 90 UTC dates: the old request-ID scheme can create about 90 warning rows; this design creates one, then retains only the compact suppression state after content expiry. This is an illustration, not a production measurement.

## 9. Dashboard and admin behavior

Expose lifecycle, desired enablement, independent chat restriction/grant health, current incident/remedy, observation freshness, and recovery progress through versioned owner/projection/manage contracts. UI labels distinguish **Enabled**, **Dormant**, **Bot banned — disabled**, **Timed out until …**, **Action required**, **Disabled by you**, **Reconnecting after unban…**, and **Connection check failed** for exhausted transient recovery. Dormant copy explains that the bot wakes automatically; it must not look disconnected.

The dashboard must show **Bot banned — disabled** immediately after the durable ban fact is committed and the status cache/SSE is invalidated. This status survives notification read/dismissal/expiry and must not collapse to generic Disabled merely because `is_active=false`. A known ban remains visible alongside missing-grant information; a read outage shows the last confirmed status as stale rather than asserting recovery. If the owner subsequently disables the bot, show **Disabled by you** with the retained ban as a secondary condition and explain that unban will not auto-enable it.

Keep one actionable status panel for the current issue. A confirmed ban says “Unban the bot in Twitch. It will reconnect automatically” when both control listeners are healthy. Hide normal Restart/Enable-as-toggle while the ban remains; provide an explicit “I unbanned it — retry” verification fallback and a Disable control to cancel automatic resume. Missing permission/listener copy explains that automatic recovery is unavailable and offers reconnect consent or the verification fallback. Unban changes the status to **Reconnecting after unban…** until verification completes. Revoked authorization says Reconnect; ambiguous access failure uses neutral wording. Failed repair reports its result inline. Admin shows ban source/time, timeout expiry, grant health, last meaningful use/check, listener availability, and pending cleanup failures. Update the shared connection-state mapper, dashboard actions, and warning translations together. Passive inspection never refreshes Twitch or changes activity.

Deploy decoder and UI support before writing new lifecycle values. In particular, [services.ts](../../web/dashboard/src/lib/server/services.ts) currently maps unrecognized subscription states to `unenrolled`, and [the home loader](../../web/dashboard/src/routes/(app)/+page.server.ts) auto-enrolls active/unenrolled accounts. Unknown lifecycle/cache loss must remain unknown; remove passive auto-enrollment and route genuine first enrollment through onboarding/Enable. Preserve the existing bounded 30-second post-action connection polling; it reads stored progress and stops when hidden, terminal, or timed out.

## 10. Migration and rollout

1. Start implementation from a baseline containing PR #1246 and its successor fixes; confirm the required regression coverage exists. Add durable lifecycle, outbox, notification metadata, indexes, versioned contracts, and consumers behind feature gates. Deploy UI decoding and safe missing-state semantics first. Observe counts and query cost without changing admission.
2. Backfill in cursor-based batches. Preserve explicit disconnects; use verified registry failure reasons to seed blocked incidents. Conflicting/missing legacy facts become unknown pending explicit repair or bounded migration reconciliation, never automatic enablement. Seed activity observation from migration time when no trustworthy evidence exists.
3. Seed existing blocked incidents as already announced/suppressed before replacing the daily sender. Where a known legacy warning exists, retain the newest matching item and remove only verified duplicate automated warnings after a migration dry-run. Daily IDs alone do not prove that historical warnings belong to one continuous incident; uncertain history can expire normally. Do not send catch-up warnings to the entire inactive population.
4. Switch all connection-warning producers together to incident IDs; disable the go-live beacon on every replica. Deploy recovery evidence contracts and authorized listener enrollment before subscription cleanup; verify their interest is preserved. Enable lifecycle gates at enqueue and execution. Confirm legacy producer traffic has reached zero before advancing. Existing grants without recovery permission keep explicit repair and must not be shown guaranteed automatic unban recovery.
5. Enable dormancy for a small cohort after the observation period, exercise every wake path, then expand. Apply cache expiry/index pruning only after durable recovery and missing-cache behavior pass validation. Roll out batched cleanup and deletion handling independently of dormancy.

Rollback may disable automatic dormancy and keep healthy dormant accounts event-driven. It must retain incident suppression, explicit-disable protection, lifecycle decoding, and execution gates. Returning to the old daily reminder path or default-enabled cache-miss behavior is not an acceptable rollback. Schema additions remain readable until old work has drained.
